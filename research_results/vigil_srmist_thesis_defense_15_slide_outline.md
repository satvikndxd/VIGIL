# VIGIL Thesis Defense: 15-Slide SRMIST Academic Panel Outline

**Audience:** SRMIST thesis-defense panel  
**Narrative arc:** Hook → SOTA illusion → VIGIL methodology → brutal truth → clinical root cause → Phase 4 vision  
**Presenter stance:** A rigorous engineer defending leakage-safe negative results, not an accuracy headline.

## Cover

**Slide title:** VIGIL: Interpretable Temporal Deep Learning for ECG Arrhythmia Classification

**Subtitle:** A leakage-safe, record-level study of generalization failure and representation learning

**Presenter information:** SRMIST Computer Science Thesis Defense

**Visual direction:** A clean single-lead ECG trace transitioning into a record-level split diagram; restrained clinical blue with orange highlights.

**Speaker notes:**

Good morning. This work is called VIGIL: Interpretable Temporal Deep Learning for ECG Arrhythmia Classification. The core contribution is not a claim that we solved ECG diagnosis. It is a rigorous evaluation framework that asks a harder question: can a model recognize arrhythmia morphology in a patient record it has never seen? I will show that when we enforce this constraint, many attractive model improvements fail. That failure is scientifically valuable because it tells us where the actual generalization bottleneck is and motivates a disciplined Phase 4 representation-learning experiment.

## Slide 1

**Slide title:** The Real Question Is Generalization

**Visual direction:** A beat-level split schematic contrasted with a record-level split schematic.

**On-slide bullets:**

- ECG morphology is strongly patient and record specific.
- Useful models must transfer beyond observed recording styles.
- VIGIL evaluates unseen records, not shuffled beat fragments.
- OOD reliability matters more than attractive in-distribution accuracy.

**Speaker notes:**

I begin with the evaluation target because it determines every conclusion in this thesis. ECG beat morphology is not generated by a stationary source. It changes with patient anatomy, conduction pathways, lead placement, signal quality, medication state, and local rhythm context. If the model trains and tests on different beats from the same record, it can exploit patient-specific style instead of learning a transferable arrhythmia representation. VIGIL therefore treats the record as the statistical unit. The model must confront a genuinely unseen recording style at test time. This is an out-of-distribution problem, and it is much more difficult than recognizing shuffled beat fragments from a familiar patient.

## Slide 2

**Slide title:** The SOTA Illusion Begins With Leakage

**Visual direction:** Comic-strip panel: same superhero in two costumes versus a villain with similar colors; beside it, “random beats” versus “held-out records.”

**On-slide bullets:**

- Random beat splits leak patient morphology across partitions.
- Leakage can produce fake 98%+ “SOTA” accuracy.
- The model recognizes style before pathology.
- Record splits expose the true transfer problem.

**Speaker notes:**

This is what I call the SOTA illusion. A random beat-level split can place neighboring beats from the same record into training and test sets. The model then sees the same patient-specific baseline morphology, noise signature, electrode behavior, and rhythm context on both sides of the split. The comic-book analogy is useful here. Random splits are like recognizing a superhero by their color palette because the same artist drew every panel. A record-level split forces the model to recognize that superhero when another artist draws them in a completely different style. High random-split accuracy is not automatically false, but it does not establish OOD generalization. VIGIL chooses the harder interpretation deliberately.

## Slide 3

**Slide title:** VIGIL Defines an Honest OOD Baseline

**Visual direction:** Compact metric card row over a locked-split pipeline: records → train/validation/test → model → metrics.

**On-slide bullets:**

- Fixed record split: 31 train, 7 validation, 10 test.
- Held-out test set contains 4,154 sequences.
- No record leakage and no subject leakage.
- Baseline Macro-F1 is 0.4012, not a headline accuracy.

**Speaker notes:**

The central baseline is an RNN augmented with RR and handcrafted morphology features. It is evaluated on a fixed record-level split with 31 training records, 7 validation records, and 10 held-out test records. The audit confirms zero record overlap and zero subject overlap. Its held-out Macro-F1 is 0.4012, Macro-AUPRC is 0.5963, and Macro-AUROC is 0.7906. I do not present 0.4012 as a low score compared with leakage-contaminated random-split papers. I present it as an honest OOD baseline. It tells us exactly how much work remains when the model is denied shortcuts based on patient identity and recording style.

## Slide 4

**Slide title:** The Data Contract Locks the Science

**Visual direction:** A pipeline diagram: MIT-BIH record → annotation mapping → 90/90 window → robust normalization → eight-beat context → locked split.

**On-slide bullets:**

- MIT-BIH mapped to N, S, V, F, and Q.
- Each target beat uses a 90/90 sample window.
- Robust median/MAD normalization prevents scale domination.
- The test set never changed during Phases 1–3.

**Speaker notes:**

Before comparing models, I locked the data contract. The MIT-BIH annotations are mapped into five classes: N, S, V, F, and Q. Each target beat is extracted using a 90-sample pre-window and a 90-sample post-window. The model receives eight-beat temporal context and robust median/MAD normalization. Most importantly, the test split is fixed before experimentation and never changed. That means a candidate cannot win by benefiting from a new partition. It must win by learning a better representation under the same OOD challenge. This discipline is what lets negative results remain interpretable rather than becoming artifacts of shifting evaluation conditions.

## Slide 5

**Slide title:** Handcrafted Morphology Carries the Baseline

**Visual direction:** Three-column ablation chart: waveform only, waveform plus RR, waveform plus RR plus morphology.

**On-slide bullets:**

- Waveform-only RNN reached 0.2665 Macro-F1.
- Adding RR alone dropped Macro-F1 to 0.1886.
- RR plus morphology increased Macro-F1 to 0.4012.
- Explicit morphology outperformed small learned representations.

**Speaker notes:**

The first positive result was a representation result. A waveform-only RNN achieved Macro-F1 0.2665. Adding RR features alone reduced it to 0.1886, so timing features by themselves were not enough. When I added RR plus handcrafted morphology, Macro-F1 increased to 0.4012. The morphology block includes beat amplitude, energy, RMS, slope, width, QRS proxy, RR interval, previous RR, RR ratio, local heart rate, and local RR variability. The lesson is subtle: the small recurrent encoder did not reliably extract the discriminative morphology from raw waveform data alone. Explicit signal-processing features supplied useful inductive bias. But they improved the aggregate score without fixing the difficult N and F failures.

## Slide 6

**Slide title:** More Capacity Did Not Create Generalization

**Visual direction:** Parameter-count versus Macro-F1 scatterplot, with RNN and attention variants labeled.

**On-slide bullets:**

- LSTM, GRU, Bi-LSTM, and CNN variants were tested.
- Bi-LSTM attention reached 0.7953 Macro-AUROC.
- No architecture exceeded the final morphology baseline.
- Complexity cannot replace record-diverse supervision.

**Speaker notes:**

A natural hypothesis was that the baseline failed because it was too simple. I tested RNN, LSTM, GRU, Bi-LSTM, Bi-LSTM with attention, and CNN plus Bi-LSTM plus attention. The attention model reached a relatively strong Macro-AUROC of 0.7953 in the waveform architecture comparison, but it did not deliver a decisive Macro-F1 gain or recover N and F recall. The CNN plus Bi-LSTM plus attention model was substantially worse. This is an important engineering result: additional parameters and fashionable components are not a substitute for learning the correct invariances. If the training records do not expose enough morphological diversity, a larger model can become better at fitting record style rather than better at transferring across records.

## Slide 7

**Slide title:** The Brutal Truth Is Per-Class Failure

**Visual direction:** Large normalized confusion matrix with N→V and F→S/V cells highlighted in orange.

**On-slide bullets:**

- S recall reached 0.9373; V recall reached 0.9733.
- N recall remained 0.0000 across the retained baseline.
- F recall was 0.0833 on only 12 test beats.
- Aggregate metrics conceal clinically relevant asymmetry.

**Speaker notes:**

This is the slide where the aggregate headline is no longer sufficient. S recall is 0.9373 and V recall is 0.9733. But N recall is zero, and F recall is only 0.0833 with a test support of 12 beats. The confusion matrix shows that N is routed mainly to S and V, while F is absorbed by S and V. I do not hide these numbers, because they are the most important constraints in the thesis. Macro-F1 is valuable because it prevents high performance on S and V from dominating the evaluation, but even Macro-F1 cannot replace per-class inspection. A model that fails a class completely is not rescued by a good average.

## Slide 8

**Slide title:** The Failure Is Not Simple Class Imbalance

**Visual direction:** Original-symbol composition table transformed into a train/validation/test Sankey or stacked bars.

**On-slide bullets:**

- Mapped N has 1,200 training and test examples.
- Original-symbol N has zero validation examples.
- F has only 12 held-out test sequences.
- Symbol composition shifts across records and partitions.

**Speaker notes:**

The simple class-imbalance story is incomplete. At the mapped-class level, N has 1,200 training examples and 1,200 test examples. Yet N recall is zero. The key is that mapped labels compress multiple original annotation symbols and record-specific morphologies into one category. Original-symbol N has no validation support in this split, while certain S and Q symbols have radically different support across partitions. F is also intrinsically unstable because there are only 12 held-out test sequences. The error mechanism is therefore a coupled problem: cross-record domain shift, original-symbol composition shift, weak morphology representation, and sparse validation support. Oversampling the mapped class cannot necessarily teach the model a morphology it never sees in the right domain context.

## Slide 9

**Slide title:** Why Normal Beats Become Ventricular Predictions

**Visual direction:** Annotated waveform comparison: narrow normal QRS, aberrantly conducted sinus beat, PVC; label width, slurring, slope, and terminal forces.

**On-slide bullets:**

- Aberrant sinus conduction can widen and slur QRS morphology.
- Bundle-branch refractoriness can alter normal beat appearance.
- Wide aberrant QRS can resemble a PVC mathematically.
- Unseen patient baselines amplify N-to-V confusion.

**Speaker notes:**

The N-to-V error has a plausible biomedical signal-processing explanation. A normal sinus impulse does not always produce a narrow, stereotyped QRS. If conduction encounters a refractory bundle branch, the beat can be conducted aberrantly, producing a bundle-branch-block-like waveform: broader QRS duration, slurred depolarization, altered terminal forces, and changed slope-energy structure. A premature ventricular complex can also produce a broad, unusual QRS. In the learned feature space, especially for a patient whose baseline morphology was never seen during training, those beats can be close. Our handcrafted features explicitly measure width, energy, RMS, slope, and a QRS proxy. That is exactly why the confusion is mechanistically plausible. This is not a clinical reannotation claim for individual samples; it is a principled explanation of the observed decision geometry.

## Slide 10

**Slide title:** Interventions Exposed the Real Constraint

**Visual direction:** Diverging bar chart of Phase 2 and Phase 3 candidate Macro-F1 versus retained baseline.

**On-slide bullets:**

- CNN morphology RNN fell to 0.1217 Macro-F1.
- Symbol-aware sampling fell to 0.2863 Macro-F1.
- Symbol-aware loss fell to 0.1823 Macro-F1.
- Domain adversarial training collapsed to 0.1272 Macro-F1.

**Speaker notes:**

This is the negative-results slide, and I treat it as central evidence rather than a footnote. Learned CNN morphology encoders underperformed dramatically. Symbol-aware sampling and symbol-aware loss also degraded the model. The domain-adversarial CNN/RNN reached Macro-F1 0.1272, indicating that forcing the representation to forget record information was not benign under this capacity and training budget. A plausible explanation is gradient interference: the task objective needs discriminative morphology while the adversarial branch pushes the encoder away from domain information that may still be entangled with useful signal structure. The takeaway is not that these methods are universally wrong. It is that, under our exact OOD setting, they did not solve the problem and therefore cannot be promoted.

## Slide 11

**Slide title:** One Candidate Improved F Recall—At a Cost

**Visual direction:** Pareto frontier with Macro-F1 on one axis and Macro-AUPRC on the other; λ=0.25 and λ=0.50 marked.

**On-slide bullets:**

- Multi-task λ=0.25 achieved F recall of 0.1667.
- λ=0.25 remained below baseline Macro-F1: 0.3999.
- Multi-task λ=0.50 reached Macro-F1 of 0.4186.
- λ=0.50 reduced AUPRC to 0.5825 and AUROC to 0.7709.

**Speaker notes:**

The multi-task original-symbol head is the most interesting near-miss. Its loss combines the five-class cross-entropy with an auxiliary original-symbol cross-entropy weighted by lambda. At lambda 0.25, F recall increased to 0.1667 and AUPRC slightly improved, but Macro-F1 remained below the retained baseline. At lambda 0.50, Macro-F1 increased to 0.4186 and F recall remained 0.1667, but Macro-AUPRC fell to 0.5825 and Macro-AUROC to 0.7709. That is a real trade-off, not a clean victory. The replacement gate requires higher Macro-F1, no major ranking degradation, improved N or F recall, and seed stability. The model does not pass that conjunction. This is exactly why a rigorous gate matters: it prevents us from declaring a winner based on one favorable metric.

## Slide 12

**Slide title:** Generalization Must Be Measured, Not Assumed

**Visual direction:** Record-level heatmap with records 108, 104, 200, and 122 called out; bootstrap interval glyphs.

**On-slide bullets:**

- Record 108 Macro-F1 was approximately 0.0083.
- Record 200 Macro-F1 was approximately 0.3337.
- Five-seed baseline Macro-F1 was 0.4087 ± 0.0174.
- Bootstrap Macro-F1 interval was 0.3924 to 0.4094.

**Speaker notes:**

Aggregate OOD performance is itself heterogeneous. Record 108 is nearly a complete failure with Macro-F1 around 0.0083, while record 200 is substantially better at roughly 0.3337. This spread tells us that a global score is averaging over distinct domain difficulties. I also repeated the retained model family across five seeds: Macro-F1 is 0.4087 plus or minus 0.0174. The 500-resample bootstrap interval for the final held-out Macro-F1 is 0.3924 to 0.4094. Those analyses strengthen the baseline as a reproducible control, but they do not cure N/F failure. The technical lesson is that robustness includes variance across seeds, uncertainty across examples, and heterogeneity across records—not just one test statistic.

## Slide 13

**Slide title:** Phase 4 Learns Morphology Before Labels

**Visual direction:** SimCLR 1D pipeline: one train-record waveform branches into two ECG augmentations, shared encoder, projection head, NT-Xent attraction/repulsion, then classifier fine-tuning.

**On-slide bullets:**

- Pretrain on train-record waveforms without class labels.
- Create two ECG views from each source beat.
- Attract positive pairs; repel other batch examples.
- Fine-tune the encoder on locked N/S/V/F/Q supervision.

**Speaker notes:**

Phase 4 is our next hypothesis, not a result. The idea is self-supervised contrastive learning in the SimCLR style. We take one train-record waveform and create two conservative ECG-specific views using baseline wander, amplitude scaling, and protected temporal masking. A shared 1D residual encoder maps both views into representations. The NT-Xent loss pulls the two views of the same source beat together and pushes other batch examples apart. The comic-book analogy returns here: a superhero should still be recognized in a classic suit or a stealth suit, but should not be confused with a villain that happens to share a color palette. For ECG, the desired invariance is to recording nuisance—not to diagnostic morphology. The projection head exists only for contrastive learning; the encoder representation is then fine-tuned for five-class classification. Phase 4 still has to earn replacement: pretraining uses train records only, and any candidate must be compared with the matched supervised CNN and final handcrafted baseline, then pass seed, bootstrap, calibration, robustness, and per-record checks.

## Closing

**Slide title:** The Thesis Contribution Is Honest Generalization

**Visual direction:** One final statement over a simple ECG trace: “Measure the hard problem. Learn from failure. Replace only with evidence.”

**On-slide bullets:**

- VIGIL replaces accuracy theater with OOD accountability.
- Negative results located the real representation bottleneck.
- Phase 4 converts that bottleneck into a falsifiable hypothesis.
- Thank you. Questions and technical discussion welcome.

**Speaker notes:**

My final claim is not that VIGIL has produced a clinically deployable arrhythmia classifier. It has not, and this thesis does not make that claim. The contribution is an honest research framework: a locked record-level split, exact mapping and leakage audits, explicit per-class failure reporting, seed and bootstrap uncertainty, calibration and robustness analysis, and a replacement gate that refuses to promote a model on selective evidence. The negative results are not dead ends. They show that the real bottleneck is representation transfer across records and original-symbol composition shifts. Phase 4 turns that diagnosis into a falsifiable self-supervised learning experiment. Thank you, and I welcome questions on the split design, the failure analysis, the trade-offs, or the Phase 4 protocol.

## Panel Q&A Backup: High-Value Responses

| Likely panel question | Technical response |
|---|---|
| Why not report accuracy? | Accuracy is dominated by the easiest classes and hides complete N/F failures; Macro-F1 and per-class recall expose this asymmetry. |
| Why is 0.4012 acceptable? | It is not “acceptable” clinically; it is an honest OOD research baseline under strict record-level evaluation, unlike leakage-prone random splits. |
| Why did you retain the weaker-looking baseline? | It was the only candidate satisfying the strongest measured aggregate ranking profile without a gate-complete trade-off. |
| Why not oversample N and F more aggressively? | Symbol-aware sampling and loss degraded held-out results, showing that frequency correction alone did not solve morphology/domain shift. |
| Why SimCLR instead of another larger classifier? | The evidence points to representation invariance, not raw capacity; Phase 4 tests that hypothesis under the same gate. |
| Does Phase 4 use test data without labels? | No. Any test-waveform exposure, labeled or unlabeled, would invalidate the OOD generalization claim. |
